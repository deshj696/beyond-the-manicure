Beyond the Manicure

Nail technicians as micro-designers of identity, labor, and experience.

This project treats nail design as a full design process, moving from interview and user research to freestyle sketching, 3D modeling, and final render. By making the process visible, we argue that nail work is not simply beauty service, but embodied, user-centered design.

01 / Initial Question → Shift

From proof to system.

The research question changed shape once the appointment became visible as a designed sequence.

Research frame

Are nail technicians designers?

How does design operate through identity, space, labor, and the body?

Our project shifted from proving nails are design to showing the entire nail appointment as a design system. In the earlier concept, we described this as a move from object to experience: from the final nails alone toward skill, aesthetic judgment, interaction, space, intention, and relationship.

03 / Interview Insights

Lauren Efron described nail work as reading the person, not just decorating the hand.

“You can gain an entryway to their vibe based on how they present themselves.”

Interpretation: nail design begins as user research. The technician reads style, mood, body language, and self-presentation before translating identity into color, texture, length, and risk.

“A successful design to me is not so much in the actual nails, but it's in their reaction.”

Interpretation: the final product is not only an object. Success happens through embodied response: confidence, recognition, delight, and the feeling of being understood.

“Instagram is my version of a portfolio... When I post, I'm putting out what I want to come back.”

Interpretation: the nail tech is also a creative director and business owner, designing not only sets but the public image of a practice and the future clients it attracts.

“I like to create my own work, my own design... so that when I give someone a set, it's an Efron nail set.”

Interpretation: Lauren frames nail work as authored design, not copy-paste decoration. The set carries a recognizable design language and point of view.

“There’s different thought processes that go into not only each nail, but each set.”

Interpretation: the set is a system. Each nail has its own concept, but the order, rhythm, contrast, and hand-level composition are also designed.

“I try and create really good relationships with all of my clients on a personal level, with boundaries.”

Interpretation: the appointment is interpersonal design. Conversation, trust, care, and boundaries shape the experience as much as color or material.

04 / Client Interview

Lauren became the client: The Editorial Maximalist.

Format

Reusable press-ons, not super long.

The final set needed to be wearable and removable, but still expressive enough to feel like a designed object.

Visual Language

3D, editorial, attention-grabbing.

Lauren said 3D elements give “more room to play with design,” which pushed the set toward sculptural, high-impact micro-objects.

Aesthetic

Asymmetrical, maximalist, visually dense.

She said she would wear anything “as long as there’s a lot going on visually,” so the set uses contrast, rhythm, and different concepts across the hand.

Process

Freestyle first, interview-led.

To stay true to Lauren’s practice, we did not build the final set from a moodboard. We used her interview as the brief and embraced a freestyle model.

06 / The Nail Set

Five micro-objects, one wearable interface.

Each nail acts as a micro-object and a wearable interface. We chose this concept to match Lauren’s maximalist design language: mixing digi-pop, cowgirl, rock-pop, and Y2K aesthetics; varying nail shapes through star cuts, rounded squares, and flat squares; using pastel pink as a summer theme; and expanding her approach to miniaturization and object recontextualization.

Five concepts waiting under the surface.

09 / Closing

What we see is the nails. What is actually designed is the interaction, the identity, the labor, and the experience. If this is not design, what is our definition of design missing?
